# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome! This repository organizes and standardizes project management practices for OctoAcme.

## Overview of Project Management Processes

OctoAcme adopts a structured and people-centered approach to project management, focusing on iterative delivery, clear ownership, and maximizing customer value. Every project is guided from initiation through planning, execution, deployment, and continuous improvement. The typical lifecycle starts by validating ideas, aligning with stakeholders, clarifying success criteria, and defining a high-level project outline. Approved initiatives are decomposed into detailed backlogs, release plans, and risk registers, emphasizing transparency and clarity from the outset.

Core roles in the OctoAcme process include the Project Manager, Product Manager, Developers, and QA/Testing. The Project Manager ensures delivery, manages schedules and risks, and facilitates communication, while the Product Manager drives product vision, prioritization, and stakeholder alignment. Developers are responsible for implementing features and maintaining technical excellence, collaborating with QA/Testing, who verify quality and acceptance criteria. Each role has clear responsibilities to ensure accountability and successful cross-functional collaboration.

Workflows are standardized via project boards that track work from backlog through to completion, and pull requests (PRs) must meet specific conventions such as linking to issues, including acceptance criteria, and passing automated tests and code reviews. This encourages manageable and incremental code delivery, with strong emphasis on peer review and continuous integration. Quality assurance is further reinforced by automated unit, integration, and end-to-end testing, security scans, and mandatory manual QA for critical features, all of which precede deployment.

Communication is structured yet adaptable: teams participate in daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, and regular demos or reviews. Stakeholders receive scheduled updates and ad-hoc escalations flow through predefined paths—from the team up to sponsors as needed. Risks are tracked and regularly reviewed using a risk register, and after every sprint, release, or incident, teams hold retrospectives to capture learnings and prioritize actionable improvements. This routine encourages both accountability and a culture of continuous, measurable improvement.
